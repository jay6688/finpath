# FinPath — PROJECT BRIEF

**Version:** v0.1  
**Status:** DRAFT  
**Created:** 2026-08-19  
**Product Type:** Beginner-first financial learning web platform  
**Primary Platform:** Responsive Web / PWA  
**Primary Language:** English-first, Chinese explanation available on demand

---

## 1. Product Purpose

FinPath is a beginner-first financial learning platform designed for people who are interested in money and investing but do not know where to begin.

It should help a user move gradually from:

Personal Finance Basics  
→ Saving  
→ FD / Retirement Savings  
→ Inflation  
→ Risk & Return  
→ Investing Basics  
→ ETFs  
→ Stocks  
→ Financial Statements  
→ Company Analysis  
→ Valuation  
→ More Advanced Investing Topics

The platform should combine:

- guided learning;
- real financial data;
- company exploration;
- financial news;
- paper investing;
- progress tracking;
- gamification;
- an adaptive AI finance tutor.

The core goal is NOT to tell users what to buy.

The core goal is:

> Help users understand money and investing well enough to make their own informed decisions.

---

## 2. Primary User

### Initial target

Absolute or near-absolute beginners.

A typical first user may:

- understand basic saving concepts;
- know products such as FD or retirement savings;
- have heard of stocks or investing;
- possibly already have a brokerage account;
- still feel unsure about using real money;
- not understand terms such as Revenue, FCF, P/E, ROE, ETF or Balance Sheet;
- feel overwhelmed by existing finance apps and learning materials;
- not know what they should learn first.

FinPath should assume:

> Curiosity exists, but financial vocabulary and confidence may not.

---

## 3. Product Philosophy

### Beginner first

Never assume financial knowledge that has not been introduced.

A beginner should be able to open any important page and find:

> What is this?

> Why does this matter?

> Explain in simpler English.

> Explain in Chinese.

### One clear next step

The user should never need to plan an entire learning journey by themselves.

The default home experience should clearly surface:

> **Your next step**

while still allowing free exploration.

Learning Path = navigation.

Learning Path ≠ restriction.

Users may explore companies, news or concepts ahead of their current learning stage.

### Learn through real things

Do not separate “learning finance” and “using finance tools” completely.

Whenever possible:

Concept  
→ Real example  
→ Real company/data/news  
→ Explanation  
→ Optional exploration

Example:

Inflation  
→ Malaysia inflation story  
→ Why purchasing power changes  
→ How it may affect FD / stocks / consumers

## 4. Main Product Areas

### 4.1 Home

Purpose:

Tell the user what matters right now without overwhelming them.

Potential elements:

- Only One Next Step
- Weekly Momentum
- Current Learning Path
- Continue Learning
- Today's Story
- Explore a Company
- Paper Portfolio
- Tutor shortcut
- recent achievements

Avoid an information-dense financial terminal on the beginner homepage.

### 4.2 Learn

Structured learning path beginning with personal finance fundamentals.

Early learning route may include:

1. Money & Saving
2. Emergency Fund
3. FD
4. KWSP / Retirement Basics
5. Compound Growth
6. Inflation
7. Risk & Return
8. What Investing Is
9. Asset Classes
10. ETFs
11. Stocks
12. How Companies Make Money
13. Financial Statements
14. Basic Company Analysis
15. Valuation

Lessons should normally be short and focused.

Typical target:

~5–10 minutes.

No mandatory exams.

### 4.3 Explore

Search and understand real investments.

Initial focus:

US-listed companies.

Later expand toward:

- Malaysian stocks;
- ETFs;
- global markets;
- bonds;
- indices;
- currencies;
- commodities.

A company page should support two information densities.

#### Beginner Mode

Answer:

- What does this company do?
- How does it make money?
- Is the business growing?
- Is it profitable?
- Does it generate cash?
- Does it have significant debt?
- What should I investigate next?

#### Advanced Mode

Eventually include:

- financial statements;
- multi-year trends;
- margins;
- free cash flow;
- ROE / ROIC;
- valuation ratios;
- quarterly vs annual results;
- SEC filings;
- peer comparison;
- more advanced metrics.

Each important metric should provide educational context.

### 4.4 News

Do NOT create an endless finance-news feed.

Home/news recommendations should emphasize understanding over consumption.

A useful starting mix:

- Malaysia
- Global Economy
- Company / Markets

Each important story should support:

#### What happened?

Plain-language summary.

#### Why this matters

Economic / financial significance.

#### Concepts involved

Example:

Interest Rates  
Inflation  
Currency  
Margins  
Consumer Demand

#### Related assets / companies

Show relationships without presenting automatic BUY / SELL conclusions.

The editorial inspiration may include contextual financial journalism such as The Economist, but FinPath must not imitate or reproduce another publisher's content.

### 4.5 Practice / Paper Portfolio

Allow users to experiment without real money.

Key beginner scenario:

#### My First RM500

Use realistic small-capital scenarios rather than defaulting only to unrealistic RM100,000+ virtual portfolios.

Possible choices may include:

- cash;
- emergency fund;
- FD;
- ETF;
- individual stock.

Help the user understand differences in:

- liquidity;
- risk;
- expected return;
- time horizon;
- diversification.

Paper investing should teach decision-making.

It should NOT gamify frequent trading.

### 4.6 AI Finance Tutor

The Tutor should act as a teacher, not a stock-picking oracle.

Example questions:

- What does P/E mean?
- Why did profit increase but the stock fall?
- Explain this chart to a beginner.
- What should I investigate next?
- Explain this in Chinese.
- I don't know what this means.

Tutor behaviour:

1. Adapt explanations to the user's current knowledge.
2. Prefer simple English first.
3. Offer Chinese when requested or useful.
4. Avoid unnecessary jargon.
5. Connect new concepts to concepts already learned.
6. Encourage reasoning instead of blindly giving conclusions.
7. Do not pretend to predict future market performance.
8. Do not automatically give BUY / SELL recommendations.

The Tutor should maintain a lightweight learning profile.

Example:

Revenue — Familiar  
Free Cash Flow — Introduced  
P/E — Not introduced  
ETF — Learning  
Financial Statements — Not started

## 5. Learning Progress

Do not use school-style grades.

Avoid:

FAILED  
7 / 10  
Retake Quiz

Possible concept states:

Not Seen  
→ Introduced  
→ Used  
→ Revisited  
→ Familiar

Progress should preferably be inferred from actual use rather than requiring users to manually maintain a study tracker.

## 6. Gamification

FinPath may use strong gamification.

However:

> Gamify learning, not trading.

Possible systems:

- XP
- Levels
- learning paths
- achievements
- badges
- collections
- Knowledge Chests
- cosmetics/themes
- weekly missions
- progression maps
- milestones

Reward actions such as:

- completing a learning concept;
- revisiting an older concept;
- researching a company;
- understanding the first financial statement;
- writing the first investment thesis;
- exploring different asset classes;
- understanding a financial news story.

Do NOT reward:

- excessive trading;
- number of trades;
- short-term portfolio profits;
- risky bets;
- speculative behaviour.

---

## 7. Motivation System

Do not make daily streaks the central motivation mechanism.

Prefer:

### Weekly Momentum

Examples:

Learning sessions this week: 2  
Concepts explored: 4  
Companies researched: 1

Missing one day should not create a feeling of failure.

FinPath should encourage returning, not punish absence.

---

## 8. Soft Guardrails

The platform should help beginners slow down before acting without blocking exploration.

Example:

User attempts to paper-buy a company they have never researched.

FinPath may say:

> You haven't looked at how this company makes money yet.

> Want a 3-minute overview first?

Options:

[Show overview]  
[Continue anyway]

The platform advises.

The user decides.

## 9. Personal Finance Profile

Personalisation may use approximate financial ranges rather than unnecessary exact values.

Examples:

Available cash:

- < RM1,000
- RM1,000–5,000
- RM5,000–20,000
- RM20,000+

Monthly amount available to save:

- < RM100
- RM100–500
- RM500–1,000
- RM1,000+

Emergency fund:

- None
- < 1 month
- 1–3 months
- 3–6 months
- 6+ months

Existing financial products:

- Cash
- FD
- KWSP
- ETF
- Stocks
- Other

Exact values should not be required when a range is sufficient for teaching.

## 10. Privacy Principles

Privacy is a core product requirement.

### Data minimisation

Collect only information needed to provide the feature.

### Approximate before exact

Prefer financial ranges when exact values provide little additional educational value.

### Local-first where practical

Sensitive personal-finance information should remain on-device whenever practical.

### Optional cloud sync

Cross-device learning continuity is important.

Users should eventually be able to log in and synchronise selected data such as:

- learning progress;
- watchlist;
- concept history;
- paper portfolio;
- notes;
- achievements.

Do not automatically upload every piece of personal-finance information.

### Demo Profile

Provide a demo / presentation mode containing fake financial data.

Useful for:

- portfolio demonstrations;
- screenshots;
- interviews;
- public demos.

### V1 must NOT request

- banking passwords;
- brokerage passwords;
- NRIC;
- card details;
- private banking credentials;
- trading credentials.

## 11. Real Money Boundary

FinPath should initially stop before trade execution.

Supported journey:

Understand  
→ Explore  
→ Compare  
→ Simulate  
→ Build a thesis  
→ Understand risk

Then:

User decides independently whether to act through their brokerage.

No automatic trading in early versions.

## 13. Responsive Product Strategy

FinPath must treat desktop and mobile as first-class environments.

### Desktop

Best for:

- company analysis;
- financial statements;
- larger charts;
- comparisons;
- detailed research;
- portfolio analysis.

### Mobile

Best for:

- short lessons;
- news;
- AI Tutor;
- watchlist;
- progress;
- quick company snapshots;
- paper portfolio checks;
- continuing learning during short free periods.

The mobile version must NOT simply be a desktop dashboard shrunk onto a small screen.

### Product Shell

### Research / Company Analysis

Higher information density and more professional financial presentation.

Minimal, calm, spacious and friendly.

Visually engaging but secondary to financial credibility.

## 16. Anti-AI Design Principle

The product should not visually look like a generic AI-generated dashboard.

Avoid blindly defaulting to:

- excessive gradients;
- glowing everything;
- glassmorphism everywhere;
- identical rounded cards;
- random 3D finance illustrations;
- generic robot mascots everywhere;
- unnecessary animations;
- filler marketing copy;
- decorative elements with no information purpose.

Before implementation:

1. Research real production products.
2. Identify useful patterns.
3. Build an intentional design system.
4. Define typography, spacing, palette and information hierarchy.
5. Define one distinctive visual signature.
6. Critique the design.
7. Only then implement it.

Real product references may include categories such as:

- financial research tools;
- investing apps;
- learning products;
- news products;
- modern SaaS dashboards;
- mobile finance apps.

Do not clone any single product.

## 21. V0 Scope

V0 is NOT the full FinPath vision.

V0 should prove one complete vertical slice.

### Suggested V0

User opens FinPath.

User can:

1. see a clean responsive homepage;
2. search `AAPL`;
3. identify Apple;
4. retrieve real public financial data;
5. display Revenue;
6. see several years of Revenue history;
7. open: **What does Revenue mean?**
8. receive a beginner-friendly explanation;
9. see: **Why does this matter?**
10. use the page correctly on desktop and mobile.

That is enough for V0.

Do not build:

- authentication;
- cloud sync;
- full gamification;
- AI Tutor backend;
- full paper trading;
- global markets;
- dozens of ratios;

before the first vertical slice works.

---

## 22. V0 Success Criteria

V0 succeeds if:

- real AAPL data loads reliably;
- source and financial period are visible;
- a complete beginner can understand what Revenue represents;
- desktop UI is usable;
- mobile UI is usable;
- architecture is clean enough to extend;
- no unnecessary sensitive information is collected;
- the design does not feel like an obvious AI dashboard template.

Most importantly:

> If a beginner leaves understanding one financial concept better than before, the first product loop works.

## Appendix A — Approved V0 Amendments

**Approved:** 2026-08-19

1. Accept the `Next.js + FastAPI` two-application architecture.
2. Accept `Learning Margin / Annotated Financial Record` as the V0 design direction.
3. At the product layer, guarantee complete support only for Apple/AAPL in V0. Keep the underlying ticker-to-CIK mapping general and do not needlessly hard-code Apple. The UI must clearly state the current Apple/AAPL limitation rather than pretending to support an all-market search.
4. Use SQLite only to cache public SEC data; do not save user information in V0.
5. Add `sources` / provenance to local Revenue teaching content so definitions are reviewable and traceable.
6. Configure the SEC `User-Agent` through environment variables. Do not commit a private email address or personal contact information.
7. Keep the FinPath request rate materially below the SEC's published limit and use caching.
8. The AAPL integration test must validate the full FY2025 Revenue provenance chain: value, fiscal year, period end, form, filed date, accession, and SEC source URL.
9. Do not expand V0 scope while implementing these amendments.
